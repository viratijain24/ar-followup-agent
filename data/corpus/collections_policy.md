# Accounts Receivable Collections Policy

This policy tells the finance team how to follow up on unpaid customer invoices. Every reminder
must be accurate, courteous, and proportionate to how late the payment is. Our goal is to get
paid while keeping the customer relationship healthy.

## 1. Overdue stages
<!-- stage: reference -->
"Days overdue" counts calendar days after the invoice due date, using the outstanding balance
(invoice total minus payments received). An invoice is in exactly one stage:

| Stage | Days overdue | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Not due | 0 or fewer | No reminder. |
| Grace period | 1 to 6 | No reminder. Payments are often in transit. |
| Friendly reminder | 7 to 29 | Send a friendly reminder. |
| Firm reminder | 30 to 59 | Send a firm reminder; copy the account manager. |
| Final notice | 60 or more | Send a final notice; escalate as described in section 5. |

## 2. Friendly reminder (7 to 29 days overdue)
<!-- stage: friendly -->
Tone: warm, brief, and assume good faith. The customer has most likely overlooked the invoice.
Open with a polite greeting and use the contact's name. Phrase the reminder as a helpful nudge,
for example: "Just a quick reminder that invoice INV-XXXX appears to be outstanding." Offer to
resend the invoice or help if there is an issue on their side. Do not mention late fees,
escalation, or consequences at this stage. Keep the email under 150 words.

## 3. Firm reminder (30 to 59 days overdue)
<!-- stage: firm -->
Tone: polite but direct and businesslike. State clearly that the invoice is now more than 30
days past due and ask for payment or a firm payment date within 7 days. Ask the customer to
reply if there is a problem with the invoice so it can be resolved. You may state that late
fees may apply under the payment terms (see section 6), but do not calculate or quote a fee
amount. Copy the account manager. No threats and no legal language.

## 4. Final notice (60 or more days overdue)
<!-- stage: final -->
Tone: serious, formal, and respectful, never aggressive or insulting. Title the email "Final
notice". State the number of days overdue and the outstanding balance, and request payment
within 7 days. State that if payment or a payment plan is not agreed by then, the account will
be escalated to our finance director and further services may be paused. Late-fee wording from
section 6 may be included. Do not threaten legal action and do not mention collection agencies;
only the finance director may decide on those.

## 5. Escalation
<!-- stage: final -->
At the final-notice stage, the reminder must be reviewed by a human before it is sent, and the
customer's account manager must be informed. If an invoice reaches 90 days overdue, the finance
director decides on next steps (payment plan, service pause, or external collection). The
automated system must never send anything beyond a final notice on its own.

## 6. Late fees
<!-- stage: firm -->
Our standard terms allow a late-payment charge of 1.5% per month on overdue balances. Approved
wording: "Under our payment terms, a late-payment charge of 1.5% per month may apply to overdue
balances." Never state a calculated fee amount in a reminder: fees are only invoiced separately
after approval by the finance director. Late fees are never mentioned at the friendly stage.

## 7. Required content in every reminder
<!-- stage: always -->
Every reminder must include: the invoice number, the original invoice total, the outstanding
balance, the due date, and how to pay (bank transfer to Example Bank, account 000123456789,
quoting the invoice number). Every amount, date, and invoice number in a reminder must match our
records exactly; never estimate, round, or invent a value. Use the invoice's own currency.

## 8. Partial payments
<!-- stage: all -->
If the customer has paid part of the invoice, thank them for the payment and state the amount
received and the remaining balance. Ask for the remaining balance, not the original total.

## 9. Disputes
<!-- stage: all -->
If a customer has disputed an invoice (for example, claiming work was incomplete or the amount
is wrong), stop automated reminders for that invoice. Do not send a payment reminder. Route the
invoice to the account manager for human review until the dispute is resolved in writing.

## 10. Promised payment dates
<!-- stage: all -->
If a customer has promised to pay by a specific date, do not send a reminder before that date.
If the promised date passes without payment, send a reminder that refers to the promise politely
and continue at the stage that matches the days overdue.
